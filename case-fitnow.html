Cases/FitNow

Turning installs into first bookings for a fitness subscription

FitNow gives one subscription to 250+ sport and wellness studios in Kyiv, but only 2 of every 100 people who installed the app reached a first booking. With a teammate, I researched how people really choose a class and redesigned the path from the first open to the first booking.

01

Context & problem

FitNow is one subscription to 250+ sport and wellness studios in Kyiv. People installed the app, but only 2 of every 100 reached a first booking. They left before they understood why one subscription beats a regular gym membership. The client asked us to rethink the path from the first open to the first class.

02

What I did

A client project at Projector, May to August 2026, in a team of two product designers with Olena Polishchuk. We shared it end to end: funnel audit, interviews and synthesis, hypotheses, flows, UI kit, prototype testing and the client presentation.

03

What we found

In the live app
  • 11 steps before the home screen, some asking for data like gender and Instagram
  • No visible Book button on the schedule
  • Points nobody understood instead of clear prices
In the interviews
  • People choose by route: home, work, a kindergarten
  • Google Maps is the habit for searching
  • Nobody wants to start alone
04

3 bets we designed

We ranked 8 hypotheses with ICE and took three into design and testing:

  1. Onboarding that pays back every answer. Who it is for, interests, where and how you travel. The answers build a personal home feed.
  2. The map is the search. Studios sorted around saved places and free time.
  3. Bring a friend to the same class. One link from the class page, bonuses for both.
05

Outcome

We tested all three flows in 20 sessions with both audiences and handed FitNow the flows, a UI kit turned into a design system, and the metrics to track for each bet. The activation result will be measured by the FitNow team after release.

06

What I learned

Every onboarding question has to pay the user back. If an answer doesn't change what people see next, it's a reason to leave.
